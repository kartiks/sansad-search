# SansadSearch — Personas

**PRD version:** v1.0

---

## P1 — Academic Researcher

**Role and context:** PhD student or faculty in political science, history, constitutional law, or public policy. Works in a university or think tank. Uses SansadSearch for primary-source research — tracing legislative history, tracking a member's statements across sessions, comparing debates across time periods including Constituent Assembly proceedings.

**Goals:** Retrieve records matching precise parameters (speaker, session, proceeding type, date range). Cross-reference CA debates with modern legislative history. Cite and verify sources.

**Pain points:** Systems that expand queries without transparency; results that mix proceeding types without filtering; imprecise speaker attribution; inability to save and return to complex searches.

**Interaction style:** Keyboard-heavy. Uses advanced search extensively. Filters by multiple dimensions simultaneously. Expects exact parliamentary terminology to work (e.g., "calling attention motion", "unstarred question", "private member bill"). Saves searches and revisits them across sessions.

**Key scenarios:**
1. Filter all starred questions by a specific minister across the 17th Lok Sabha to analyze policy positions over time.
2. Search Constituent Assembly debates for "fundamental rights" filtered by speaker and date range.
3. Save a multi-filter search (body + date range + proceeding type) and return to it the following week.

---

## P2 — Journalist

**Role and context:** Reporter or editor covering Parliament, politics, or policy. Works on deadline. Uses SansadSearch to verify claims, locate a politician's exact statement, or establish when an issue was first raised in the House.

**Goals:** Find a specific record fast. Confirm the exact wording of a statement. Get the source link for citation. Get in and out quickly.

**Pain points:** Slow systems; too many irrelevant results; no direct link to the original record; ambiguous snippet truncation that obscures key phrases.

**Interaction style:** Plain-language queries first, then refines if needed. Cares most about the snippet and source link. Uses sorting (newest first) frequently to find recent debates. Rarely uses advanced search but benefits from query expansion catching alternate spellings and synonyms.

**Key scenarios:**
1. Search a minister's name + a policy topic to find their most recent statement on the floor.
2. Sort results by newest first to find the most recent debate on a bill.
3. Follow the source link to the official Sansad or Rajya Sabha record for direct citation.

## P3 — Law Student / Lawyer

**Role and context:** Law student or practicing advocate researching constitutional history, bill debates, or legislative precedents. Frequently uses Constituent Assembly debates for constitutional interpretation arguments. May also track amendments to specific legislation across sessions.

**Goals:** Find authoritative primary sources. Distinguish precisely between legislative bodies and proceeding types. Build a research trail that can be cited in legal submissions.

**Pain points:** Mixed results from different bodies or proceeding types without clear labeling; inability to filter to CA debates specifically; no way to save a research trail for later use.

**Interaction style:** Precise terminology, uses advanced search filters regularly (legislative body, proceeding type, date range). High value on speaker attribution accuracy. Saves searches with full filter state to return to later.

**Key scenarios:**
1. Filter to Constituent Assembly debates only, search for "right to property", filter by speaker.
2. Find all debates on a specific bill number across both Houses including committee stage.
3. Save a search with multiple active filters to resume research in a subsequent session.

---

## P4 — Engaged Citizen

**Role and context:** Educated general public — student, activist, or interested layperson with no specialist parliamentary knowledge. Searches in plain language to understand what Parliament has said about a topic they care about.

**Goals:** Find relevant debates and statements on a topic. Understand what was said and by whom. Follow a source link to read more.

**Pain points:** Jargon-heavy interfaces; results that require knowing parliamentary procedure to interpret; unclear why certain results appear; no way to understand what the system searched for on their behalf.

**Interaction style:** Single plain-language query, rarely uses filters, benefits most from query expansion and clear highlighted snippets. Reads the snippet before deciding whether to follow the source link. Does not use advanced search in first session; may use recent search history to revisit a topic.

**Key scenarios:**
1. Search "debates on MGNREGA" and read highlighted snippets to find the most relevant results.
2. Notice the query expansion notice ("also searching for: employment guarantee, rural employment") and understand why results include those terms.
3. Follow a source link to read the full debate on the official government website.
