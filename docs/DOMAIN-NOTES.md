# Domain Persona

Assume expertise in Indian parliamentary procedure and constitutional law, including the working of the Constituent Assembly of India (1946–1950) and the ongoing legislative processes of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha.

## Parliamentary Procedure and Debate Types

Understand and apply the following distinctions across all agents:

**Questions**
- **Starred questions** — oral questions to which ministers give answers on the floor of the House; supplementary questions are permitted after the main answer
- **Unstarred questions** — written questions to which written answers are laid on the table; no supplementary questions allowed
- **Short notice questions** — oral questions on matters of urgent public importance with less than the standard notice period

**Special procedures**
- **Zero hour** — informal convention immediately after Question Hour (12 noon); members raise matters of urgent public importance without prior notice; not in the Rules of Procedure but established by convention
- **Adjournment motion** — motion to adjourn the business of the House to discuss a definite matter of urgent public importance; requires leave of the House and is highly restricted in Rajya Sabha
- **Calling attention motion** — member calls the attention of a minister to a matter of urgent public importance; minister makes a brief statement; no debate follows
- **Short duration discussion** — discussion on a matter of urgent public importance raised by a member; no formal motion or vote; limited to two and a half hours
- **Private member bill** — bill introduced by a member who is not a minister; distinct from government bills introduced by ministers
- **Privilege motion** — motion alleging breach of privilege of the House or a member

**Budget and legislative process**
- Budget Session, Monsoon Session, and Winter Session are the three regular sessions; special sessions may also be convened
- Bills may be introduced in either House (except Money Bills, which originate in Lok Sabha)
- Rajya Sabha cannot amend or reject Money Bills; it can only make recommendations

**Presiding officers**
- **Lok Sabha** is presided over by the Speaker (and Deputy Speaker in absence)
- **Rajya Sabha** is presided over by the Vice President of India as Chairman (and Deputy Chairman in absence)
- **Constituent Assembly** had a President (Dr. Rajendra Prasad) and a separate Constituent Assembly (Legislative) for interim legislation

## Structure of Official Parliamentary Records

- Lok Sabha debates and questions: published at sansad.in; available as HTML and PDF by session, date, and type (debates, questions, bills)
- Rajya Sabha debates and questions: published at rajyasabha.gov.in; similar structure
- Constituent Assembly debates (1946–1950): 12 volumes, available via Lok Sabha archives; structured differently from modern records — no question hour, no starred/unstarred distinction; proceedings are plenary debates on the draft Constitution and interim legislation
- Records include: date, session number, sitting number, volume/part, speaker attribution, subject matter, and in modern records, the type of proceeding (question, debate, bill stage, etc.)
- Data formats vary by era: older records are scanned PDFs; modern records are structured HTML and searchable PDFs

## Information Retrieval and Search

Assume expertise in:
- Full-text search systems and relevance ranking (BM25, TF-IDF, vector search)
- Query expansion: synonym expansion, stemming, spell correction, phonetic matching
- Faceted search and metadata filtering patterns
- Passage-level vs. document-level retrieval tradeoffs for long-form legislative text
- Search UX conventions: autocomplete, highlighted snippets, pagination, sorting
- Web scraping practices for government websites: rate limiting, robots.txt compliance, HTML/PDF parsing, structured data extraction

## Users

Users include researchers, academics, journalists, lawyers, law students, and engaged citizens. They range from domain experts (who know precise parliamentary terminology) to general users (who search in plain language). The interface must serve both without requiring expertise.
